Unmoderated Usability Testing

HeartMath App — First-Time User Testing

I ran an unmoderated usability study to understand how brand-new users experience the HeartMath app in their very first session — before they've learned what "heart coherence" even means.

MethodUnmoderated, think-aloud
FocusFirst-time experience
PlatformiOS mobile app
Study Goal

The goal was to understand how first-time users experience the HeartMath app when they try it for the first time. Participants were asked to download and open the app, create an account, explore naturally for a few minutes, and think out loud while doing it.

Specifically, I wanted to understand: what users think the app is for, whether they understand the idea of heart coherence, which features they explore first, and how they react to sessions, measurement, and pricing.

Overall Impression

Most users understood that the app is related to stress management and relaxation. However, many struggled with the core concept of heart coherence and how the app actually works. Some had a positive experience once they started a guided session — others felt confused or overwhelmed early on.

Behavioral Patterns Observed

Rather than starting a session right away, most users spent time trying to understand the app and its purpose first. Six consistent patterns emerged.

01

Users first scan the home screen

Most didn't start a session immediately — they scrolled Daily Practice, Programs, Techniques and Courses first to understand what the app offers.

02

"Learn" is used to decode the app, not to learn

Users opened Learn not to start a course, but to figure out what heart coherence means before engaging with any exercise.

03

"Start Here" is the natural first click

Most participants clicked Start Here first, reading it as the recommended entry point — though some abandoned it mid-session to keep exploring.

04

Exploring wins over committing

Some users deliberately avoided a full session so they could keep exploring and give more feedback during the test.

05

Unexpected screen movement during sessions

During Start Here, the screen auto-scrolled toward the header. Users weren't sure if they'd caused it — breaking the calm the session was meant to create.

06

Curious but skeptical about measurement

The camera-based heart measurement drew attention but also doubt — accuracy, wearable support, and camera privacy all came up unprompted.

Users approached the app with curiosity but also uncertainty. Once they actually experienced a session, perception turned more positive — the challenge is helping them reach that moment sooner.

Key Findings

"Heart coherence" isn't immediately clear

Users grasped stress reduction, but not how heart coherence fits in. Some guessed breathing or meditation — none felt confident.

"I have no idea what heart coherence means."

The measurement feature raises questions

Users questioned how a phone camera detects heart rate, whether it's accurate, and raised privacy concerns about the camera.

"I don't understand how it measures heart rate without a monitor."

Friction during account creation

A disabled Create Account button, unclear checkboxes, and vague field feedback caused small moments of frustration right at the start.

The app feels content-heavy at first

Learn, Measure, Play, Programs, Courses and Techniques all competing for attention on first open.

"There's a lot going on. It's a lot to absorb."

"Start Here" is a strong entry point

Most users clicked it first and read it as the intended starting point — though a few stopped early to keep exploring elsewhere.

Guided sessions were a positive experience

Voice guidance, breathing visuals, and the calming tone all landed well with users who completed one.

"The voice was very soothing. I feel calmer."

Subscription appears too early

Some hit a free-trial prompt before they'd explored enough to decide, creating hesitation.

"Most of the things I wanted to try required the free trial."

Pricing feels high for some

Several felt the monthly price was expensive and preferred annual — and wanted reassurance about cancelling the free trial.

Visual design was well received

The calm color palette, soft gradients and readable text were called out positively — the design supported the emotional tone the app is going for.

Home Screen — Full View

The screen most participants scanned before doing anything else — Daily Practice, Guided Techniques, Training, and Learning all stacked on first open.

Major Opportunities

→ Clearly explain heart coherence — simple language, a short animation, and science references

→ Improve first-time onboarding — what it is, how it measures, a 1-minute try, then explore

→ Increase trust and scientific credibility — research references, testimonials, expert credentials

→ Simplify the home screen for new users — highlight Start Here, Daily Practice, Learn Basics only

→ Offer more free experiences before the subscription screen — a few sessions, one game, one course

Key Takeaway

The app provides powerful stress-relief experiences, but many users don't reach that moment during their first interaction. Unclear concept framing, skepticism about the measurement feature, a content-heavy home screen, and early subscription prompts all slow the journey down. Fix onboarding, concept clarity and trust in measurement, and the app has strong potential to deliver real value to people looking for stress management and relaxation tools.
