IBM Rational · HCL Collaboration

CQ Dashboard — turning a static test-tooling screen into a workspace people could actually configure.

Rational Test Control Panel's dashboard was rigid, one-size-fits-all, and slow to update. The brief: design a widget-based, user-configurable dashboard that different roles could shape around their own work — from the ground up.

01 · Discover

Research split across two continents

Discovery for the dashboard was led onsite in Italy, where the team could sit with primary customers directly. I supported the research remotely from India — synthesising inputs, feeding back questions to shape the next round of conversations, and turning raw findings into structured personas and scenarios.

Prior product & builder/user separation context

The pain points were consistent no matter who we asked: performance and stability, no single sign-on, a static dashboard nobody could configure, long waits for changes to commit, and no way to hide what wasn't relevant.

02 · Define

Two people, two very different relationships with the dashboard

Persona

Tom — Quality Assurance

Motivations

Tackle business risk and cost concerns; adequate test automation coverage; wider adoption of test lifecycle metrics.

Frustrations

Communication gaps between testers, dev, management and clients; keeping automation scripts current and reusable.

Ideal Experience

Versioned, collaborative automation; access to enterprise-wide dev/ops tools in one place.

Persona

Michael — Customer Support

Motivations

Establish a centre of excellence; provide 24x7 coverage; prevent user issues from ever reaching his desk.

Frustrations

Too much "bleed through" of user problems; no way to know the actual state of the system.

Ideal Experience

"Dashboard tells me the exact state of the system" — with advance notice before issues surface.

As-is: what their day actually looked like

"Tom wants to automate a test scenario that leverages SV by locating stubs and an environment to do this in. Having achieved this he just wants the stub to be running and know that it is fulfilling his needs."

Manually finding a suitable stub/environment was difficult; no way to tell if a virtual service was actually running.

"Michael wants more knowledge in the hands of users, so he can focus on managing the system and ensuring the smooth running of things."

Constantly asked to check if a stub was really running, with no confidence or easy way to find out.

05 · Design

From boxes-and-labels to a working visual system

The wireframes locked the flow: log in, land on an empty state if you had nothing set up yet, build a board through a short wizard (details → tabs & layout → widgets → users), then land on a fully personalised dashboard. Below is that flow taken to high fidelity, in the dense, data-forward visual language of IBM's Rational tooling.

06 · Reflect

Being on this project end-to-end — through cross-geo research, persona work, prioritisation, structure and design — was a different kind of ownership than supporting a single phase. The hardest part wasn't any one screen; it was keeping Tom's and Michael's very different needs legible through every later decision, from a feature-priority matrix down to what a single widget on the final board actually showed.
