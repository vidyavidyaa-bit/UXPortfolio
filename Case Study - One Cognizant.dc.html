Cognizant · Enterprise Internal Tools

Insights DevOps — giving a dev-led tool a real information architecture.

Cognizant's internal DevOps insights platform had been built out feature-by-feature by engineering, with no UX behind it. I led the redesign with one junior designer alongside me, working to the new UUX 2.0 guidelines.

Client — Cognizant Role — Senior UX Lead, with one junior designer Method — Application review + competitor benchmarking
Making the Case for Research

There was no schedule for user interviews on this one, and no existing research to inherit. Rather than guess, I made the case to stakeholders for secondary research — competitor benchmarking and published market data on what users expect from this category of tool. Once that was approved, my junior designer and I split the work: he ran a structured audit of the existing application screen by screen, while I focused on synthesising market research and shaping the competitor comparison.

The project had been driven by development, not experience — nobody had asked what the information architecture should be.

Application Review

My junior designer led a screen-by-screen audit of the existing Traceability Dashboard — where filtering lived, how results surfaced, and where the tool asked users to hold too much in their head at once.

Redesigning the Drill-Down

The Traceability Dashboard was the clearest example of a dev-led IA: filter, then a flat list, with no summary in between. We restructured the results into a Summary view — a shared status row plus per-tool cards for JIRA, Git, Jenkins and Sonar — before a user ever has to open the detailed pipeline table underneath.

Summary view — the answer before the detail.

Pipeline view — the detail is still one click away, per tool, when it's actually needed.

Observation Notes From the Application Review

Dashboard

Dashboard list isn't upfront, increasing cognitive load. Recent-dashboard view isn't useful on first login.

Navigation

Submenu isn't highlighted per selected screen; users don't want a hidden menu.

Logo

Both the Cognizant and Insights logos together weren't recommended by most users.

UI

Complete UI customisation was one of the top criteria expected by users.

Final Solution

Main Organization — grid and list, both configurable

Reflection

Leading this with a junior designer meant the work wasn't just mine to do — it was mine to structure. Splitting the application audit from the market synthesis let us both move fast without secondary research turning into an excuse to skip rigor. Not every project gets primary research; this one taught me how to make a smaller research budget count.
