Usability Testing

HR Application — Beneficiary Nomination

The HR team built an application for associates to nominate beneficiaries during their employment with Cognizant. I ran moderated usability testing to validate it before rollout.

Participants12 associates
GeographyIndia
ChannelDesktop
Satisfaction Score7.6 / 10
Objective
  • Do associates find it easy editing primary & dependent details?
  • Do associates find adding dependent details straightforward?
  • Do associates understand how to interact with the screen to complete percentage of shares?
  • Do associates view the feedback to complete the task, or skip the pending task?
Key Insights

"Overall, the application is good and simple to start with, but needs a few tweaks."

  • Most associates skipped reading "About the application," and the "Get Started" call to action wasn't prominent.
  • Some had initial difficulty understanding the application, but once they got the hang of it, they set their goals successfully.
  • All tasks were completed without major barriers, though a few needed help along the way.
  • Some labels weren't relevant to completing certain tasks — worth revisiting for clarity.

Detailed Findings

Landing Page

Finding: Associates didn't read "about the application" on the landing page and got confused how to proceed; "Status: Yet to Start" was mistaken for a link.

Recommendation: Enhance content visually with infographics/carousel and make "Get Started" prominent once read, or surface it via an "About" icon in the header. Make Primary & Dependent details the landing page.

Navigation & Page Heading

Finding: Back arrow and page heading shown together implied the back arrow led to "primary & dependent details"; Add/Edit links and editing dependent details were confusing.

Recommendation: Keep the back arrow and heading visually separate. Rename "Add" to "Add dependent" and place it top-right; give each dependent its own edit icon.

Content — Primary vs. Dependent Fields

Finding: Fields like "Gender" and "Marital Status" were ambiguous — associate's or father's? Adding a dependent required clicking "Edit" under primary details.

Recommendation: Clear demarcation of associate vs. father details in separate rows; contextual section headings like "My Details" and "My Dependents."

Content — Dependent Status & Type

Finding: "Dependent status – Active/Inactive," "Dependent Beneficiary type," the sudden "Guardian" field, and the "Disabled" toggle all confused associates.

Recommendation: Keep an info icon near each label; rename to "Beneficiary?" (Yes/No); show guardian guidance inline when age is under 18; rename "Disabled" to "Physically Challenged."

Content — Dependent Address

Finding: Associates expected "same as above" address to auto-fill on click; "Country" felt misplaced; "Date of Death" felt out of place on a dependent profile.

Recommendation: Make it a deferred create — enable address type on selection. Move "Country" after "Postal Code." Move "Date of Death" to its own section, renamed "Demise / Expired."

Content, Presentation & Navigation

Finding: "Same phone as employee" read ambiguously; associates couldn't find where to add "National ID"; and weren't sure if the form was actually saving.

Recommendation: Rename to "Same phone number as employee." Give National ID its own section with a clearer add affordance. Validate and highlight missing mandatory fields per section as the user fills them in, rather than only on Save.

Percentage of Share

Finding: Associates couldn't find where to add "Percentage of Share," and weren't sure if "Next" would lead there. Some guidance text was requested for terms like Provident Fund and Gratuity.

Recommendation: Rename the button to "Add Percentage of Share" for contextual naming. Add an info icon with a short description per component.

Pending Task

Finding: The pending task list didn't feel like it required immediate action.

Recommendation: Highlight the affected fields directly in "Primary & Dependent details" so users act in place, rather than routing to a separate fixes screen.

Good to Have Features
  • Address associates as "Associate" rather than "Employee."
  • Add "Widowed/Separated" to the Marital Status dropdown, beyond Married/Single.
  • Add an LGBTQ option to the Gender dropdown, while keeping privacy intact.
  • Show the process as a sequential flow — Primary & Dependent details, then Percentage of Share — to avoid back-and-forth navigation.
  • Offer a PDF download for each component while previewing under "Percentage of Share."
  • When a dependent Spouse is added, auto-update Marital Status instead of surfacing it as a pending task.
