01 Website Revamp

Rebuilding the homepage from research up

Led a full revamp of the HeartMath website, starting with competitive research into how wellness brands present their products online.

What the current homepage got wrong

Continuous scroll, no anchors

No decision points — users stayed passive readers instead of taking action.

Science before experience

Explained the product before letting users feel any value — no early emotional payoff.

No single primary CTA

Competing CTAs ("Shop", "Learn More", "Support") left users unsure where to go.

Flat content hierarchy

Every section carried equal visual weight, with no prioritized storytelling.

Dense blocks of text

Long paragraphs with little visual breakup meant key messages got skimmed past.

Emotionally neutral UI

Scientifically credible, but didn't evoke the calm the product is meant to deliver.

Competitive research

Benchmarked five wellness and wearable products to understand how they guide users emotionally and cognitively, not just how they market.

Oura Fitbit Enterprise Calm Headspace Muse
Value is clear in 5 seconds

Oura, Calm, and Headspace lead with one outcome-based message — visuals over text.

Emotional state designed first

Calm and Headspace design tone and pacing before comprehension — emotion precedes explanation.

Experience preview before commitment

Oura, Muse, and Headspace show the product experience — breathing animations, score visuals — before asking for trust.

Science supports, doesn't lead

Muse, Oura, and Fitbit surface evidence after value is established, with depth on demand.

Homepage design & usability testing

Designed the new homepage around these patterns — a single primary CTA, an early experience preview, and science moved in support of the story rather than ahead of it — then ran usability testing to validate the direction before handoff to development.

Usability testing — key findings (5 participants, unmoderated)

High severity
No clear "how it works" explanation

Users didn't understand what the device does or how "coherence" applies to them — the page needed a short explanation up front.

High severity
Not enough product detail to justify a purchase

Users wanted a clearer breakdown of how the product actually helps with stress before committing.

Medium severity
Pricing was hard to find

Cost was a recurring question — users wanted pricing, subscription vs. one-time purchase, and value justification stated clearly.

Medium severity
Too much text, not enough visuals

Users skimmed rather than read, and asked for short videos, diagrams, and demos over paragraphs.

What changed as a result

Added a "how it works" visual explainer near the top, ahead of the science deep-dive.

Surfaced pricing and FAQ answers earlier instead of hiding them behind extra clicks.

Rebalanced the page toward visuals — icons, diagrams, and short demos in place of long text blocks.

02 Email Design System

From one-off emails to a reusable module system

Marketing was building every campaign email from scratch — Holiday Sale, Summer Sale, Spring Sale — with no shared system. I designed a modular email design system on HubSpot so most of each build could assemble itself.

The problem

Every seasonal campaign (Holiday, Summer, Spring) was designed and built independently — slow, inconsistent, and hard to scale.

A reusable module library would let marketing assemble new campaigns without a designer touching every email.

The system

Built a HubSpot-native module library — headers, product grids, CTAs, footers — that automated roughly 80–90% of each new email build.

The only manual step left: the campaign banner, designed in Figma and handed off per send.

The result

Campaign turnaround dropped from a full custom build to a banner swap — freeing design time for higher-impact work.

One system now powers every seasonal sale email.
